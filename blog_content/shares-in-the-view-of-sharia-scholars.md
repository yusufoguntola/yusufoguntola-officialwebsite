## Introduction

Matters of Islamic Jurisprudence (*Fiqh*) requiring a legal judgment of *Halal* (permissible) or *Haram* (prohibited) must be determined strictly through the lens of divine revelation and the established legal maxims (*Qawa'id Fiqhiyyah*) laid down by Islamic law (*Shariah*). Such determinations require a meticulous evaluation of the underlying reality (*Tahqiq al-Manat*) of contemporary financial mechanisms.

In recent times, as corporate entities like the Dangote Refinery proceed with Initial Public Offerings (IPOs), proponents of permissibility often lean on contemporary financial screening frameworks—most notably **AAOIFI Standard No. 21**. These proponents argue that:

1. Such corporations fall under the category of "mixed companies" (*Al-Sharikat Al-Mukhtalatah*).
2. Purchasing shares in mixed companies is permissible provided the impermissible activities (such as interest-bearing debt or non-compliant revenue) remain under certain financial thresholds (e.g., 30% or 5%).
3. The unlawful (*Haram*) portion of dividends can simply be cleansed through a process known as profit purification (*Tatheer*).
4. These financial standards rest upon recognized legal principles of *Shariah*.

However, the premier jurists of our era—who possess a profound mastery of jurisprudence, legal maxims, and financial transactions—have systematically dismantled these arguments. By examining the scholarly positions of **Sheikh Prof. Sulaiman Ar-Ruhaili**, **Sheikh Al-'Allamah Muhammad ibn Salih Al-'Uthaymeen**, and **Sheikh Al-'Allamah Saleh bin Fawzan Al-Fawzan**, it becomes evident that engaging in transactions laden with usury (*Riba*) cannot be legalized through any financial percentages or post-facto charitable donations.

## 2. Legal Maxims Governing Commercial Sales (*Qawa'id al-Buyoo'*)

All three scholars ground their analysis in the foundational maxims of Islamic commercial law:

* **The Baseline Maxim of Sales:**
  > **"الأَصْلُ فِي الْبُيُوعِ الإِبَاحَةُ"**  
  > *"The default principle in commercial transactions is permissibility."*
  
  As explained by Sheikh Ar-Ruhaili, a Muslim is permitted to buy and sell freely unless a clear legal prohibition (*Mani' Shar'i*) is established.

* **The Primary Cause of Legal Prohibition:**
  > **"كُلُّ مَا أَدَّى إِلَى النِّزَاعِ غَالِبًا فَهُوَ مَمْنُوعٌ شَرْعًا"**  
  > *"Whatever predominantly leads to dispute or conflict is legally prohibited."*

While permissibility is the baseline, the introduction of explicit divine prohibitions—specifically *Riba* (usury/interest)—overrides default permissibility entirely.

## 3. Refutation of the "Mixed Company" Definition and Financial Thresholds

### The True Definition of Mixed Companies vs. Purely Haram Entities
Modern proponents label corporate giants that borrow or deposit on interest as "mixed companies" (*Sharikat Mukhtalatah*) to justify retail stock trading. Sheikh Prof. Sulaiman Ar-Ruhaili dismantles this misclassification by establishing a clear legal categorization:

1. **Pure Permissible Companies (*Sharikat Naqiyyah*):** Companies whose core business, administrative management, and foundational charter (*An-Nizam al-Asasi*) are completely free from prohibited dealings.
2. **Prohibited Companies (*Sharikat Muharramah*):** Companies that trade in *Haram* goods (tobacco, music) OR whose **foundational charter explicitly permits or stipulates borrowing/depositing on interest**.
3. **Mixed Companies (*Sharikat Mukhtalatah*):** Companies whose primary business **and written charter are 100% permissible**, but whose board of directors commits a backend administrative violation by taking an interest-bearing loan.

Under Sheikh Ar-Ruhaili's definition, if an enterprise's financial structure or charter officially incorporates interest-bearing loans or interest-yielding deposits, it is **not a mixed company**; it is a **Purely Prohibited Company (*Sharikah Muharramah*)**. Therefore, classifying mega-refineries or industrial conglomerates that explicitly rely on bank loans with interest as "mixed" is a fundamental error in legal diagnosis (*Tahqiq al-Manat*).

### Refutation of the 30% Financial Threshold

Standard frameworks (such as AAOIFI) claim that if a company's interest-bearing debt or non-compliant income remains under an arbitrary threshold (e.g., 30% or 5%), the share remains permissible to trade. Sheikh Prof. Sulaiman Ar-Ruhaili explicitly refutes this position:

> **"وَأَمَّا مَا ذَهَبَ إِلَيْهِ بَعْضُ الْعُلَمَاءِ الْمُعَاصِرِينَ مِنْ أَنَّهُ إِذَا كَانَ فِي نِظَامِهَا نِسْبَةٌ مُعَيَّنَةٌ مِنَ الرِّبَا تَصْبَحُ مُخْتَلِطَةً وَهِيَ نِسْبَةُ 30% فَأَقَلَّ، فَهَذَا لا وَجْهَ لَهُ شَرْعًا، بَلْ إِذَا وَجَدَ الرِّبَا فِي شَرِكَةٍ فَهِيَ شَرِكَةٌ مُحَرَّمَةٌ وَلَوْ دِرْهَمًا وَاحِدًا."**
>
> *"And as for what some contemporary scholars have gone to—that if there is a specific percentage of interest (Riba) in its charter it becomes mixed, which is a percentage of 30% or less—**this has no basis in Islamic Law (La wajha lahu shar'an)**. Rather, if interest exists in a company, it is a prohibited company, **even if it is a single dirham**."*

When Sheikh Al-'Allamah Saleh bin Fawzan Al-Fawzan was explicitly questioned regarding these fatwas allowing low percentage thresholds, the question was posed as follows:

> **Text of Question Posed to Sheikh Saleh Al-Fawzan:**  
> **"أَحْسَنَ اللَّهُ إِلَيْكُمْ سَمَاحَةَ الْوَالِدِ، يَقُولُ السَّائِلُ: هُنَاكَ فَتْوَى لِبَعْضِ طَلَبَةِ الْعِلْمِ أَنَّهُ يَجُوزُ بَيْعُ وَشِرَاءُ أَسْهُمِ الشَّرِكَاتِ الَّتِي تَتَعَامَلُ بِالْحَلاَلِ وَفِيهَا بَعْضُ الْمُعَامَلاَتِ الْمُحَرَّمَةِ مِثْلَ مَا إِذَا كَانَتْ هَذِهِ الْمُعَامَلَةُ الْمُحَرَّمَةُ قَلِيلَةً بِنِسْبَةِ خَمْسَةٍ بِالْمِائَةِ، بِشَرْطِ إِخْرَاجِ هَذِهِ النِّسْبَةِ مِنَ الرِّبْحِ، فَهَلْ هَذِهِ الْفَتْوَى صَحِيحَةٌ؟"**
>
> *"May Allah be good to you, respected father. The questioner asks: There is a fatwa by some students of knowledge stating that it is permissible to buy and sell shares of companies that deal in lawful matters even if they have some prohibited transactions, such as if the prohibited transaction is small—at a rate of 5%—on the condition of extracting this percentage from the profit. Is this fatwa correct?"*

Sheikh Al-Fawzan strongly rejected the premised logic, emphasizing that usury cannot be neutralized by declaring its volume "small" or "tolerable":

> **"فَلا هُنَاكَ شَيْءٌ قَلِيلٌ يُتَسَامَحُ فِيهِ مِنَ الرِّبَا، مَا دَامَ فِيهِ رِبَا فَإِنَّ الْمُعَامَلَةَ بَاطِلَةٌ... وَهَذَا تَوْرِيطٌ لِلنَّاسِ فِي الْحَرَامِ."**
>
> *"There is no such thing as a 'small amount' of interest that can be tolerated! As long as there is usury in it, **the transaction is invalid (batilah)**... and [issuing fatwas permitting this] is **deceiving people into falling into what is prohibited**."*

## 4. Deconstructing the Myth of Profit Purification (*Tatheer*)

Proponents argue that shareholders can freely invest in non-compliant corporations and simply donate the percentage of dividends derived from *Riba* to charity to "purify" their wealth. The scholars address this claim directly:

### Sheikh Saleh Al-Fawzan: The Invalidation of "Purification" for Expected Riba
Sheikh Al-Fawzan cites the textual prohibitions of *Riba*—including the Qur'anic injunction:
> **"وَأَخْذِهِمُ الرِّبَا وَقَدْ نُهُوا عَنْهُ"**  
> *"And for their taking of usury while they had been forbidden from it..."* [An-Nisa: 161]

He clarifies that a Muslim cannot knowingly enter into a usurious trade with the intention of cleansing the income later through charity:

> **"وَلا تَصَدَّقْ بِالْحَرَامِ، اللَّهُ جَلَّ وَعَلا طَيِّبٌ لا يَقْبَلُ إِلا طَيِّبًا... وَيَقُولُ: أَنَا أَطْلَعُ وَأُطَهِّرُ، هَذَا بَعْدُ أَشَدُّ مِنْ الأَوَّلِ!"**
> 
> *"And do not give prohibited wealth in charity! Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, is Pure and accepts only what is pure... To know it deals in Riba and say: 'I will take it out and purify it'—this is even worse!"*

### Sheikh Sulaiman Ar-Ruhaili: Absence of Legal Obligation for Tatheer
Sheikh Ar-Ruhaili observes that imposing mandatory "purification percentages" on investors lacks textual evidence in *Shariah*:

> **"لَكِنَّ الَّذِي يَظْهَرُ لِي وَاللَّهُ أَعْلَمُ أَنَّ إِلازَامَ النَّاسِ بِهَذَا لا وَجْهَ لَهُ... بَلْ نَقُولُ إِنَّ الإِنْسَانَ إِذَا اشْتَرَى أَسْهُماً نَقِيَّةً فَالرِّبْحُ لَهُ، وَإِذَا اشْتَرَى أَسْهُماً مِنَ الشَّرِكَاتِ الْمُخْتَلِطَةِ فَالرِّبْحُ لَهُ وَإِنْ شَاءَ أَنْ يَتَصَدَّقَ فَحَسَنٌ."**
> 
> *"Obligating people with this [purification] has no basis in Shariah... Rather we say: if a person buys pure shares, the profit is his; and if he buys shares in mixed companies, the profit is his [legally], and if he wishes to give charity voluntarily, it is good."*

Instead of endorsing purification as a permanent regulatory workaround, Sheikh Ar-Ruhaili emphasizes that true legal prudence (*Wara'*) requires avoiding doubtful mixed stocks altogether, as they fall under the Prophet's ﷺ warning regarding ambiguous matters (*Mushtabihat*):

> **"فَبَيْعُ الأَسْهُمِ لِلشَّرِكَاتِ الْمُخْتَلِطَةِ وَشِرَاؤُهَا نَحُثُّ عَلَى اجْتِنَابِهِ فَإِنَّهُ أَبْرَأُ لِلدِّينِ وَالْعِرْضِ."**
> 
> *"Trading shares of mixed companies—we strongly urge avoiding it, for that is safer for one's religion and honor."*

### Sheikh Ibn 'Uthaymeen: Unintentional Exposure vs. Initial Investment
While Sheikh Ibn 'Uthaymeen allowed an investor who was *already trapped* (*Tawarrata*) in a company to calculate and dispose of the interest portion, he made it clear that **knowingly initiating an investment** in a company that deals in *Riba* is strictly forbidden:

> **"فَإِذَا عَلِمْنَا أَنَّ الشَّرِكَةَ تَعْمَلُ هَذَا وَلا بُدَّ، فَلا يَجُوزُ الاِشْتِرَاكُ فِيهَا... أَمَّا البُنُوكُ فَإِنَّ الْمُسَاهَمَةَ فِيهَا حَرَامٌ مُطْلَقاً."**
> 
> *"If we know that the company acts in this usurious manner with certainty, **it is not permissible to participate in it**... As for banks, contributing to them is **absolutely prohibited (Haram Mutlaqan)**."*

## Conclusion

The contention that stock offerings involving usurious debt structures—such as the Dangote IPO—can be rendered permissible via financial thresholds and profit purification is jurisprudentially untenable. As demonstrated by the scholarly verdicts of Sheikh Prof. Sulaiman Ar-Ruhaili, Sheikh Saleh Al-Fawzan, and Sheikh Ibn 'Uthaymeen:

1. **Riba is Non-Negotiable:** Usury is strictly forbidden (*Haram Mutlaqan*) in Islamic law regardless of whether it represents 1% or 30% of an enterprise.
2. **Arbitrary Thresholds Lack Shariah Foundation:** Mathematical thresholds (e.g., 30%) have no textual or methodological basis (*La wajha lahu shar'an*) in classical or authentic contemporary jurisprudence.
3. **Purification is Not a License to Invest:** Donating a percentage of dividend income cannot wash away the prohibition of deliberately subscribing to a company that engages in usury.
4. **Fatwas of Permissibility Mislead the Ummah:** Issuing fatwas that declare such stock purchases permissible under the guise of financial thresholds or purification is, as Sheikh Saleh Al-Fawzan declared, a form of deception (*Tawrit*) that misleads the Muslim Ummah into falling directly into *Riba*—one of the gravest sins in Islam.

For the Muslim investor seeking to protect their religion and worldly sustenance, adhering to pure (*Naqiyyah*) investments free from all usurious entanglements remains the only path supported by clear Islamic jurisprudence.
